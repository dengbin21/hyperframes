# 3D, simulations and generative pieces

Use 3D when the film earns it: an object or a set as the payoff, a product, a simulation that explains. Loading Three.js, WebGPU, p5 or canvas and the seek protocol are in `../runtimes.md`.

## Deterministic by construction

- Draw every frame from timeline time in the `hf-seek` listener; frames render out of order.
- Simulate once at load (physics, agents, particles) into arrays, then look up the state at `t`: `render(t)` stays a pure function. Heavy setup (meshes, shader compiles, the simulation) registers a `window.__hf.buildReady` promise.
- Seed every random choice; a seeded hash of the index, never `Math.random()`.

## Two routes, one camera

Depth is a decision about where the film lives, and it takes one of two routes. Both are "the interface in space" or "real objects in a real space"; pick by what the film has to show, not by which is more impressive.

| | Interface in space (CSS 3D) | Objects in a studio (Three.js) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What | the real interface as layers in depth: real captures, crops of them, pieces rebuilt in code | the product's things as objects: its logo extruded, captures on a device or cards, its own GLB |
| Good at | UI films: text stays sharp at any distance, every piece is a pickable DOM element, real layouts | hero shots, hardware, an icon as a monument, premium launches |
| Cost | no runtime, no build step; text is text | a runtime, lighting and materials to get right; text stays in the DOM over the canvas |
| Fails when | the layers are flat with a fake shadow (a slide deck with a tilt) | the middle of a frame is the dark back of a slab, which reads as a black flash |

```css
/* the camera is the wrapper; the world never moves itself */
.world { perspective: 1400px; perspective-origin: 50% 42%; }
.camera { transform-style: preserve-3d; will-change: transform; }
.layer  { position: absolute; transform: translate3d(0, 0, 120px); }
```

Tween `rotateY`/`rotateX`/`z` on `.camera` only. `perspective-origin` is the lens position: moving it is a pan, and it reads as a different camera, so set it once.

## When depth is chosen, the whole film is in space

The common failure is depth as a trick for the first two seconds: the film opens in 3D, then returns to flat cuts for the middle and the end. A depth film is one space the camera travels through.

- **Every stretch has a camera move in space.** Lists, proofs and features are stations in the same world — cards standing in depth, panels lifted off the interface, objects on the studio floor — and the camera travels from one to the next: an orbit along a row, a push past a near layer into the next, a crane over all of them. A cut is fine (on the beat), but the shot after it is also in space.
- **Transitions go through space**: fly through a layer into what is behind it, pull back until the last scene is one card among many, rack focus from near to far, whip along the row. Prefer these to 2D wipes and swaps.
- **Flat is the exception, used as contrast**: one deliberate flat beat (a slammed word, the pause before the drop), then back into space. The end may land flat — that is a landing, not an escape.
- **Plan it in the treatment**: the rhythm sheet names the camera move of every stretch and where each station sits in the world (x, y, z), so the film is one route rather than a deck with a 3D cover.
- **Check it**: snapshot a frame from every stretch; if a stretch's frame could be a flat slide, give it its move.

## A fine space

What separates "a model in a void" from a place:

- **A floor and a wall that do not end.** A photo-studio sweep (a cyc: a floor curving into a wall, no corner) in the product's ground colour, with the object's soft shadow on it. A hard horizon or a black void reads as unfinished.
- **Light with dark parts.** A warm key, a cool rim, a dark environment with two or three soft strips: metals read as metal. All-bright turns them white and weightless; no environment at all turns them black, including on white backgrounds. Turn the environment, not the object, so highlights sweep across it. A real HDR environment goes in as a file.
- **Layers in depth.** Things at three distances: something close (it passes the lens during moves), the subject, something behind. Crops of the same screenshot are real material that costs nothing. On a page, lift the interface's panels apart in z (60–400 px) and let them settle.
- **Depth of field** follows the camera's target. Keep the aperture small: blur that hides the product is worse than no blur.
- **Small life.** Objects float a few hundredths of a unit, a panel turns a degree. A still object in a moving camera is fine; a still frame is not.
- **Materials:** a device body clearcoated and slightly metallic, screens unlit so the interface keeps its real colours, a faint glass over a screen to catch the studio, logos in their own colours.
- **Bloom eats detail**: keep it to the brightest points. Glossy metal under a low light gives white streaks — roughness about 0.45 or more, environment intensity about 0.5. One NaN pixel turns a whole frame black through bloom, so clamp values that can divide by zero.

## Real material in 3D

- **The logo**: the real SVG, extruded, every filled path in its own colour. Never retype or redraw a logo as 3D text.
- **The interface**: real captures on a device or as floating cards; in a page world, the capture cut into layers; pieces that must change (typing, a reply arriving, a number counting) rebuilt in code, traced from the capture at the same size.
- **Product objects**: the product's own GLB if it has one — one file with its textures — or build from the product's real shapes (its icon paths, its screens), never a stock model.
- **Text in WebGL**: short labels on a canvas texture after the fonts are loaded; anything longer stays in the DOM over the canvas.

## How it stays right

- **Every load is awaited.** Textures, GLBs, HDRIs and fonts register a `window.__hf.buildReady` promise; the runtime waits for it before the first frame, and a missing file then fails `npx hyperframes validate` instead of rendering an empty set.
- **The WebGL frame is drawn after the seek has set every tween.** Move objects from tweened values inside the `hf-seek` handler, never from your own clock.
- **Crop coordinates come from measuring the capture's pixels** — where a panel's border or background starts — never from eyeballing a scaled preview.
- **A page world flattens** when an element between the world and a layer has `overflow` other than `visible`, a `filter`, `opacity < 1` or a blend mode: put those on the layer itself, and keep every layer in front of the camera.
- **Motion blur on fast swings** comes from the render (`data-motion-blur="on"` on the root, opt-in),
  not from a CSS filter on the canvas.

## Check it

Snapshot the dive and the payoff (`../review.md`); a frame that takes too long to draw slows every render, so profile the heaviest moment once.
